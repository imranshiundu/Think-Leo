    14→## 1. Design research — what we copied, and from whom
    15→
    16→### 1.1 How Duolingo works (and what we borrowed)
    17→
    18→Duolingo lifted its retention from ~12% to ~55% by replacing "study sessions"
    19→with a tight, high-frequency game loop (StriveCloud case study). Its engine has
    20→four gears:
    21→
    22→| Duolingo gear | What research says | Think Leo equivalent |
    23→| --- | --- | --- |
    24→| **Streaks** | A 7-day streak makes a user **3.6× more likely to stay engaged long-term** (Orizon analysis of Duolingo data) | 🔥 Streak flame with milestone freezes (§7) |
    25→| **XP & levels** | XP drives ~40% more weekly engagement; one currency feeds many meters (Ptengine case study) | XP feeds the daily ring, player level, weekly league (§6) |
    26→| **Leagues** | Social comparison without real friends = safe competition | 6 cosmetic weekly tiers: 🌱→🥉→🥈→🥇→💎→🏆 (§6.4) |
    27→| **Mascot encouragement** | Duo's emotional design keeps sessions human; rare moments feel "enormous" | Leo reacts to every answer with mood + voice bubble (§10) |
    28→
    29→**The one-currency trick** (documented in Duolingo strategy breakdowns): every
    30→lesson simultaneously advances the streak, the daily goal, the league and the
    31→XP level. In Think Leo, `addXp()` is the *only* way XP enters the system — it
    32→updates `todayXp`, `weekXp`, the streak and `xp` in one atomic action
    33→(`src/lib/store.ts`).
    34→
    35→### 1.2 How Candy Crush works (and what we borrowed)
    36→
    37→Yu-kai Chou's Octalysis breakdown credits Candy Crush's estimated **$3M/day**
    38→to stacking all 8 core drives; three matter most to us:
    39→
    40→1. **Scarcity (lives).** The 5-life limit with ~30-minute cooldowns creates a
    41→   natural stop-and-come-back rhythm — Psychology of Games calls it the reason
    42→   players *don't* burn out. → Think Leo: **5 hearts, +1 every 15 min**
    43→   (kinder for kids), plus refill tickets from world chests (§6.2).
    44→2. **The level map.** A winding trail of star-rated, locked nodes makes
    45→   progress *visible and ownable* (Core Drive 2: Development &
    46→   Accomplishment). → `LevelMap.tsx`: sine-wave trail, 3-star nodes, locks,
    47→   world banners, chests (§3).
    48→3. **The end-of-level celebration.** "Sugar Crush" turns the end of a board
    49→   into a free, escalating fireworks bonus. → **LEO RUSH**: after the 8th
    50→   puzzle, 12 seconds of ×2 points where hearts can't be lost — pure
    51→   adrenaline with zero punishment (§5.4).
    52→
    53→### 1.3 Typography (researched, not guessed)
    54→
    55→Guidance from EdTech typography research (Rubens Cantuni, *UX of EdTech*) is
    56→unambiguous: **rounded sans-serifs win for kids' products** — they read like
    57→handwriting, have tall x-heights, and no confusing double-story letterforms
    58→for early readers (Joanna Varro's early-literacy font review reaches the same
    59→conclusion). Duolingo's custom DIN-Round and Candy Crush's chunky display
    60→faces follow the same logic.
    61→
    62→| Role | Font | Why |
    63→| --- | --- | --- |
    64→| Display / headings / buttons | **Fredoka** (300–700, variable) | Chunky, bubbly, high x-height — the "Duolingo weight" |
    65→| Body / numbers / answers | **Nunito** (variable) | Rounded terminals keep digits unambiguous (`1` vs `7`, `6` vs `9`) |
    66→
    67→Both ship via `next/font/google` in `src/app/layout.tsx` (zero layout shift,
    68→self-hosted, works offline once cached).
    69→
    70→### 1.4 Sound design (the invisible mascot)
    71→
    72→Duolingo's own sound team describes the golden rule as *"one musical and
    73→sonic idea, several levels of reward"*: the combo sounds are a single family
    74→that escalates — clean hit at 5×, bigger at 10×, peak at a perfect lesson —
    75→all built on the iconic "correct answer" ding. We implemented exactly that
    76→hierarchy (§8.3). Everything is **synthesized with the Web Audio API** — no
    77→audio files — so the whole PWA stays tiny and offline.
    78→
    79→### 1.5 Ethics for kids (non-negotiable)
    80→
    81→Reward loops are sugar; a kids' app must be a vitamin. Think Leo's rules:
    82→
    83→- **No purchases, no ads, no chat, no external links.**
    84→- Hearts regen in 15 min (half of Candy Crush's 30) and levels are retryable
    85→  forever — scarcity creates rhythm, never paywalls.
    86→- Leo never shames: wrong answers get a *hint*, not a buzzer; "out of hearts"
    87→  screens suggest stretching and water.
    88→- LEO RUSH cannot cost hearts — the climax is always safe.
    89→- `prefers-reduced-motion` disables ambient animations.

    93→## 2. The core loop
    94→
    95→```
    96→            ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    97→            ▼                                              │
    98→   ┌─── HOME ────┐   tap PLAY   ┌─── PLAY (8 puzzles) ──┐ │
    99→   │ daily ring  │─────────────►│ combo × timer × Leo   │ │
   100→   │ streak 🔥   │              └──────────┬────────────┘ │
   101→   │ hearts ❤️   │                         ▼              │
   102→   └───▲─────────┘              win? ──► LEO RUSH ×2      │
   103→       │                        (fail)──► out of hearts   │
   104→       │                                │ hearts regen    │
   105→       │   XP, stars, streak, chests    ▼                 │
   106→       └──────── RESULTS: ★★★ + XP + confetti ────────────┘
   107→```
   108→
   109→One session = one level ≈ 2–4 minutes. Every exit point (results, fail, map)
   110→returns a *visible* reward or a *visible* next step — the compulsion loop, in
   111→kid-safe form.

   115→## 3. Worlds, levels & the map
   116→
   117→- **6 worlds × 10 levels = 60 levels** (`WORLDS`, `LEVELS_PER_WORLD`):
   118→  Jungle Jam → Coral Cove → Space Hop → Candy Peak → Farm Days → Road Rush.
   119→- **Unlock rule (v4 — “levels must always end”):** level *i* unlocks when
   120→  level *i−1* was **finished** — a star is the shiny version, but ANY
   121→  completed run (even 0 stars, even practice) sets the `done[i−1]` flag and
   122→  opens the door:
   123→
   124→  ```
   125→  isUnlocked(i, stars, done) =
   126→      i ≤ 2                                            // gentle start
   127→   || stars[i−1] ≥ 1 || done[i−1]                      // finish → next opens
   128→   || (i is a world's first level
   129→       && finishedLevels(previousWorld) ≥ 3)           // early world access
   130→  ```
   131→
   132→  The world gate lets curious kids peek into the next world after finishing
   133→  **3** levels of the current one (`WORLD_GATE_DONE`), instead of grinding
   134→  all 10. Finished = star or done (`finishedCount`).
   135→- **Map geometry** (`LevelMap.tsx`): nodes sit on a sine trail
   136→  `x(i) = centerX + sin(i × 1.05) × 165`, `y(i) = H − pad − (i−1) × 96`,
   137→  rendered bottom-up (level 1 at the bottom, like Candy Crush). A dotted
   138→  polyline threads the nodes; the current quest pulses and Leo stands on it.
   139→  A node a kid finished without stars shows a check mark — effort is visible.
   140→- **Level transitions:** every jump into a level plays a world-coloured
   141→  circular wipe with Leo running across (`LevelWipe` in `ThinkLeo.tsx`) —
   142→  travel is part of the game, not a route change.
   143→- **World chests** wait after levels 10/20/30/40/50/60: first 1-star clear claims
   144→  **+3 heart-refill tickets + 40 XP** (once each; `grantChest`).

   148→## 4. Question generation (the puzzle factory)
   149→
   150→### 4.1 Reproducible randomness
   151→
   152→```
   153→rng = mulberry32( hash3( levelIdx·31 + diff, attemptId, questionIndex ) )
   154→```
   155→
   156→Every number in a puzzle comes from one seeded PRNG (mulberry32 — fast,
   157→tiny, well-distributed). Same level + same attempt id ⇒ the *same* puzzle
   158→set (fair, learnable); a new attempt ⇒ a fresh set. Nothing is stored.
   159→
   160→### 4.2 The plan
   161→
   162→`buildPlan(levelIdx)` deals 8 mini-game kinds per level from a world-specific
   163→pool, with two hard rules: **no immediate repeats** and **at most one Memory
   164→puzzle per level**. Levels 1–2 always open with a Count puzzle (gentle
   165→onboarding).
   166→
   167→### 4.3 The 12 mini-games
   168→
   169→Eight thinking classics plus four hands-on games (v4) — the hands-on games
   170→exist because young kids want to **do** things, not only compute:
   171→
   172→| Kind | Skill | Difficulty scaling (diff 1→6) |
   173→| --- | --- | --- |
   174→| Count | cardinality | 3 → 16 items, tighter near-miss options (±1 → ±3) |
   175→| Maths | add/subtract | 1-digit → 2-digit → 3-term `a + b − c` |
   176→| Compare | magnitude | sticker plates → numbers up to 999; bigger *or* smaller |
   177→| Odd one | categorization | unrelated intruder → *look-alike category* intruder (fruit vs veggie) |
   178→| Sequence | pattern rules | +1 hop → doubles → triples → zigzag (+u,−d) → Fibonacci-style |
   179→| Tables | multiplication | ×2–3 → full ×12, distractors = ±one group (`a(b±1)`, `ab±a`) |
   180→| True/Trap | verification | sums ≤ 10 → ±50 with sneaky ±5 lies |
   181→| Memory | working memory | Simon-style: 3 → 6 pads, each pad has its own note |
   182→| **Tap it!** (v4) | scanning + reaction | “Tap all the monkeys!” — 9 → 16-tile mixed crowd, 3–6 targets; wrong taps only *boing*, all-found ends the puzzle with a rising scale (`found(n)`) |
   183→| **Shadow** (v4) | visual discrimination | match the animal to its silhouette; decoys come from the same world + a woodland cast (frog, fox, owl joined the sticker catalog) |
   184→| **Pattern** (v4) | pre-math logic | colour+shape tokens: AB → AAB/ABB → ABC → AABB pairs; decoys share shape *or* colour, never both |
   185→| **Feed Leo** (v4) | counting you can feel | drop snacks into Leo's bowl one tap at a time (munch SFX), take extras back, tap DONE when the count matches; bowl holds want+4 so overshoots are fixable |
   186→
   187→Judge-yourself games (Memory, Tap it!, Feed Leo) return `answer: −1` and
   188→resolve through an internal `onResult(ok)` — the reveal shows Leo's reaction,
   189→not a highlighted option.
   190→
   191→### 4.4 Distractors
   192→
   193→`nearMisses()` generates unique "close-but-wrong" answers — ±1, ±2, ±10, the
   194→classic off-by-one/off-by-ten traps — never below 0, always unique, shuffled
   195→by the same seeded rng. For multiplication the distractors are
   196→*conceptual* mistakes (one group more/less), which is what makes them fun to
   197→fall for once.

   201→## 5. The play loop: scoring, combos, Flow Ranger, LEO RUSH
   202→
   203→### 5.1 Per-question score
   204→
   205→```
   206→points = 100 × comboMult(combo) × speedMult(fracTimeLeft) × (rush ? 2 : 1)
   207→
   208→comboMult(c)    = min(3, 1 + 0.25 × (c − 1))      // ×1 … ×3 from the 9th in a row
   209→speedMult(f)    = 1 + 0.5 × f                     // answer instantly → +50%
   210→```
   211→
   212→Fast *and* streaky play is worth up to **4.5×** base. One wrong answer drops
   213→the multiplier back to ×1 — that's the adrenaline ratchet.
   214→
   215→### 5.2 Flow Ranger (adaptive difficulty)
   216→
   217→Within one attempt, per-question difficulty `diff` starts at the level's base
   218→tier (`1 + ⌊(levelIdx−1)/8⌋`, clamped 1–6) and drifts ±:
   219→
   220→```
   221→3 correct in a row  →  diff + 1   (max base + 2)   "heat up"
   222→2 wrong in a row    →  diff − 1   (min base − 1)   "cool down"
   223→```
   224→
   225→The kid never sees a difficulty number — they feel a game that breathes with
   226→them, which is the flow-channel principle (challenge rises with skill, backs
   227→off after failure).
   228→
   229→### 5.3 The clock
   230→
   231→`questionTime(diff) = max(9, 26 − diff × 2.6)` seconds. Last 5 seconds tick
   232→audibly; the last 3.5 seconds pulse a red vignette (adrenaline, not terror —
   233→the timer ring turns berry-pink, never blood-red). Timeouts count as a miss
   234→and cost a heart.
   235→
   236→### 5.4 LEO RUSH (the Sugar-Crush moment)
   237→
   238→After puzzle 8, if at least one heart survived:
   239→
   240→1. **RushIntro** (1.7 s): whoosh, gradient slam "LEO RUSH!", Leo bouncing.
   241→2. **Rush** (12.0 s): rapid-fire puzzles from `makeRushQuestion()` (2 tiers
   242→   easier so pace stays high), each worth a flat **120 pts (60 × 2)**.
   243→   **No hearts can be lost in the Rush** — it's the reward lap.
   244→3. Streak sounds escalate with every consecutive hit (§8.3).
   245→
   246→### 5.5 Stars & XP
   247→
   248→```
   249→accuracy   = correctAnswers / answeredPuzzles        (main round only)
   250→stars      = 3 if ≥90% · 2 if ≥70% · 1 if ≥50% · else 0
   251→XP         = 8 + stars × 6 + min(16, ⌊totalScore / 250⌋)   (0 XP on a failed run)
   252→```
   253→
   254→Stars gate the map; score feeds best-score badges; XP feeds everything else.
   255→A typical clear earns **26–38 XP**, so the 120-XP daily goal ≈ 3–4 levels and
   256→player levels arrive every handful of runs. Score stays deliberately big and
   257→juicy on the results screen (thousands) — XP is the *slow*, meaningful currency.
   258→Any finished run — even 0 stars — also calls `noteActivity()`, which counts the
   259→day for the streak and the play strip: effort always leaves a trace.

   263→## 6. The economy
   264→
   265→### 6.1 Player level & daily goal
   266→
   267→```
   268→xpToNext(l) = 120 + (l − 1) × 80            // gentle super-linear curve
   269→dailyGoal   = 120 XP                        // ≈ 3–4 level runs
   270→```
   271→
   272→Achievement bounties (§13.2) pay 40–200 XP each, so early levels and the
   273→first badges land inside the first session — dopamine up front, grind after.
   274→
   275→### 6.2 Hearts (scarcity, softened twice)
   276→
   277→```
   278→max = 5 · regen = 1 heart / 8 min · tokens from chests = instant full refill
   279→Leo's courage boost = 1 free full refill per local day (boostDay)
   280→learning mercy     = levels 1–2: timeouts cost NO heart (slow readers ≠ wrong)
   281→practice mode      = hearts frozen entirely, run always ends, XP halved
   282→```
   283→
   284→Regen is computed from timestamps, not tick counts: `tickHearts()` pours in
   285→`⌊(now − heartsAt) / 8min⌋` hearts whenever the app is open, and the stamp
   286→advances by exactly the poured amount, so offline time counts too. Losing a
   287→heart starts the clock only if the tank was full.
   288→
   289→**Why the changes:** the #1 kid complaint was “level 1 never ends”. The
   290→chain was: slow reader → timeout → heart lost → 0 hearts → hard stop for
   291→15+ minutes → kid (and parent) give up. v4 removes every hard stop: the
   292→daily boost, the practice mode (`Keep playing` on the out-of-hearts screen)
   293→and the 8-minute regen guarantee that a play session can always continue,
   294→and every finished run still opens the next level (§3).
   295→
   296→### 6.3 Streaks (loss-aversion, softened)
   297→
   298→On the first play of a local day — XP *or* any finished run (`noteActivity`):
   299→
   300→```
   301→lastPlayDay == yesterday → streak + 1
   302→gap = 2 days + freeze available → consume 1 freeze, streak + 1 (saved!)
   303→otherwise                → streak = 1   (first-ever play starts at 1 too)
   304→milestones: day 3 → +1 freeze · day 7 → +2 freezes
   305→```
   306→
   307→Day boundaries use the kid's own clock (`dayKey()` = local YYYY-MM-DD), and a
   308→streak only *breaks* after a full missed day without a freeze.
   309→
   310→### 6.4 Weekly league
   311→
   312→`weekKey()` = ISO week. Weekly XP resets each ISO week and maps to cosmetic
   313→tiers: Grass 0 · Bronze 100 · Silver 250 · Gold 500 · Crystal 900 · Legend
   314→1500. Calibrated to the XP economy: Bronze ≈ 2–3 playing days, Gold is a
   315→committed week. No real-player leaderboards — the opponent is yourself.

   341→## 8. The sound engine (all synthesized)
   342→
   343→### 8.1 Voices
   344→
   345→`src/lib/sound.ts` runs a lazy `AudioContext` behind a master gain, unlocked
   346→on the first pointer/key event (browser autoplay policy — documented in the
   347→UI as "sound starts after your first tap"). Voices: sine/triangle/sawtooth
   348→oscillators with ADSR-ish envelopes, plus filtered white noise for whooshes,
   349→ticks and sparkle.
   350→
   351→### 8.2 The SFX table
   352→
   353→| Cue | Recipe | Feeling |
   354→| --- | --- | --- |
   355→| `correct` | C5–E5–G5 triangle arp, 70 ms steps + air sparkle | the iconic ding family |
   356→| `wrong` | soft sawtooth A3→F3 fall, low volume | "hmm", never "NO!" |
   357→| `combo(n)` | see §8.3 | escalation |
   358→| `heartLost` | two-note minor fall | gentle "oh" |
   359→| `tick` | 30 ms 1320 Hz square | urgency |
   360→| `star(i)` | 1046/1318/1568 Hz sparkle | reward streak |
   361→| `win` | 4-note fanfare + sub-octave + shimmer | parade |
   362→| `fail` | 3-note sad wobble | sympathy |
   363→| `whoosh` | bandpass noise sweep 300→2400 Hz | ignition |
   364→| `flame` | noise crackle + rising tone | streak fire |
   365→| `pad(i)` | C4/E4/G4/C5 triangle | memory melody |
   366→| `found(n)` | rising pluck + air, pitch = hit count | tap-the-target sweep |
   367→| `munch` | two filtered noise crunches + lip pop | feed-Leo snacking |
   368→| `boing` | triangle 300→110 Hz glide | wrong tap in hands-on games (funny, never scary) |
   369→| `wipe` | falling noise sweep + thud | level-transition curtain |
   370→
   371→### 8.3 The combo escalation family (Duolingo principle)
   372→
   373→ONE motif — a major-third leap — at FOUR levels of reward:
   374→
   375→```
   376→combo 3+ : root + third                         (clean hit)
   377→combo 5+ : + fifth layer                        (bigger)
   378→combo 7+ : + octave + high-pass noise shimmer   (bigger still)
   379→combo 10+: + double-octave triplet fanfare      (perfect-streak territory)
   380→```
   381→
   382→The root also *rises with the combo* (semitone per streak, capped at 12), so
   383→long streaks literally climb a scale.
   384→
   385→### 8.4 The music (real tracks, not synth)
   386→
   387→The v2 synth loop was replaced with **licensed background tracks** after the
   388→music failed to earn its keep — the kid-facing rule: *if we can't ship a good
   389→soundtrack, ship none.* Three tracks by Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com,
   390→CC BY 4.0, credited in the Home footer), trimmed, loudness-normalized to
   391→≈ −19 LUFS and looped:
   392→
   393→| Mood | Track | Where |
   394→| --- | --- | --- |
   395→| home | "Carefree" (96 s loop) | hub & level map |
   396→| play | "Monkeys Spinning Monkeys" (121 s) | gameplay |
   397→| rush | "Sneaky Snitch" (46 s) | LEO RUSH finale |
   398→
   399→The player (`sound.ts`) crossfades between moods (~0.9 s), **ducks** to ~22%
   400→under big cues (fanfare, level-up) and rides back, pauses on tab-hide
   401→(battery manners) and retries on the next gesture when the browser blocks
   402→autoplay — the requested mood is remembered, so music *always* starts as
   403→soon as it is allowed to. Music sits under the SFX mix, never on top.

   407→## 9. Device detection — everyone plays, every visit is recorded
   408→
   409→**Policy (updated): laptops, phones and tablets all get the full game.**
   410→`detectDevice()` (`src/lib/device.ts`) still classifies the device, but the
   411→result is now used for *recording and tuning*, never for blocking:
   412→
   413→```
   414→1. mobile UA          /Android|iPhone|iPod|Windows Phone|…|iPad/
   415→2. disguised iPad     /Macintosh/ UA  &&  maxTouchPoints > 1
   416→3. touch-first        primary pointer = coarse  &&  no fine pointer
   417→4. small touch screen min(w,h) < 700 px  &&  primary pointer coarse
   418→```
   419→
   420→Rule of thumb: **a laptop's physical signature is a fine primary pointer**
   421→(mouse/trackpad). Touchscreen laptops pass; phones and tablets (including
   422→iPads pretending to be Macs) classify as `mobile`. `watchDevice()` re-checks
   423→on resize/rotation (docked tablets, rotated phones) so the recorded details
   424→always match reality.
   425→
   426→Phone-specific adaptations that ship with this policy:
   427→
   428→- the level map scales itself to the viewport (same trail, same feel);
   429→- a pocket-sized Leo rides along next to every question card;
   430→- option grids, cards and the HUD wrap cleanly down to 360 px widths.
   431→
   432→### 9.1 Device details are recorded (every visit)
   433→
   434→`src/lib/deviceInfo.ts` builds an anonymous hardware snapshot on every load:
   435→
   436→```
   437→kind (laptop/mobile) · formFactor (Phone/Tablet/Laptop/Desktop)
   438→os · browser · platform · screen · viewport · pixelRatio
   439→cpuCores · ramGB · touchPoints · language · timezone
   440→online · standalone (installed PWA?) · gpu renderer · UA (truncated)
   441→```
   442→
   443→It is stored twice: locally (`localStorage`, last 20 visits) and POSTed to
   444→`/api/device`, which validates the payload with zod and appends a
   445→`DeviceVisit` row to the SQLite database (Prisma). Reporting is throttled to
   446→twice a day per device and is strictly fire-and-forget — the game never waits
   447→on the network.

   451→## 10. Leo the mascot (state machine)
   452→
   453→Leo is parametric SVG: a 12-circle mane ring, a face, and a `Look` table per
   454→mood (`Leo.tsx`). framer-motion drives body bob, tail wag (2.2 s cycle), and
   455→blink (scaleY squash every ~4 s); paws animate independently when raised.
   456→
   457→| Mood | Eyes | Mouth | Body | Trigger |
   458→| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   459→| `idle` | open + blink | smile | float | resting / between questions |
   460→| `happy` | open | open smile | bounce | correct answer, full bowl |
   461→| `excited` | wide | open | bounce fast | combos ≥ 5, Rush, intro |

   513→## 12.1 Save-state engine contract (v3 — regression notes)
   514→
   515→Three invariants were learned the hard way and are covered by
   516→`scripts/store-test.ts` (run with `bun scripts/store-test.ts`):
   517→
   518→1. `questsForDay(day)` returns **exactly 3 quests with distinct event types**.
   519→   The save engine indexes progress by board position; two quests on one event
   520→   or a 4-row board once wiped progress on every write.
   521→2. State helpers are **pure and rebased**: `bumpQuests` / `ensureQuestDay`
   522→   take a snapshot and return a new snapshot — they never re-read `get()`
   523→   mid-transaction. (A re-read used to discard the stars `recordLevel` had
   524→   just built: levels finished, nothing saved, unlocks frozen.)
   525→3. Every XP grant and every finished run flows through `bumpPlayDay`, which
   526→   owns streak / playDays / lastPlayDay — first-ever play starts the streak
   527→   at 1, milestones mint freezes.
   528→
   529→Migration: v2 saves (inflated XP era) are rescaled ÷12 on load (guarded by
   530→the persisted `version`, so v3+ XP is never touched twice), boards are
   531→rebuilt, stars/best survive. **v4** backfills `done` from stars (old saves
   532→keep every unlocked door) and adds `boostDay`.
   533→
   534→### 12.2 v4 regression proof
   535→
   536→`bun scripts/sim-v4.ts` verifies, without a browser:
   537→
   538→1. all 40 level plans build; every one of the 12 mini-games generates a
   539→   valid, answerable question at every difficulty 1–6;
   540→2. unlock rules: levels 1–2 open, `done[i−1]` opens level *i* even at 0
   541→   stars, the 3-level world gate opens new worlds early;
   542→3. a **0-star practice finish unlocks level 2** — the “level 1 never ends”
   543→   bug is structurally impossible now.
   544→
   545→The older `scripts/store-test.ts` (quest-board contract, achievement XP,
   546→heart math) still passes unchanged.

   564→## 14. LEO POP — the merge arcade (v5)
   565→
   566→The first pure-fun game, zero maths. A Suika-style physics jar
   567→(`matter-js`) with the Think Leo cast: drop a creature, two of a kind
   568→fuse into the next, the chain ends with LEO, and two LEOs detonate into
   569→the Golden Leo jackpot. Design brain: `src/lib/arcade.ts`.
   570→
   571→### 14.1 The psychology
   572→
   573→| Hook | Mechanic |
   574→| --- | --- |
   575→| Completion drive | You saw tier N+1 once — now you NEED to build it (Suika's core loop) |
   576→| Variable ratio | Golden eggs: 12% per drop after the 4th, pity-guaranteed within 9 eggless drops — every egg pays *something*, the table only varies the SIZE of the surprise |
   577→| Dopamine ladder | Chain cascades multiply score while the screen still moves (1.15 s window); the merge sound pitch climbs with every chain step |
   578→| Collection | The Zoo Book (10 creatures) shows undiscovered silhouettes — completion compulsion |
   579→| Comeback | The first egg of each local day is guaranteed (`mergeEggDay`) |
   580→
   581→### 14.2 The numbers
   582→
   583→- Drop weights (tiers 0–4): 30/26/20/14/10 — small creatures only fall from the sky.
   584→- Merge score by created tier: `[0,1,3,6,10,15,21,28,36,45,100]`, chain multiplier `min(4, 1 + 0.5·(chain−1))`.
   585→- Golden egg outcomes: gift 34% (random tier 4–7) · party 24% (+60) · twins 22% · popcorn 20% (a whole tier pops).
   586→- Overflow line: a creature riding the line 2.2 s ends the run.
   587→- XP: `6 + min(24, floor(score/70)) + 4·newDiscoveries` — flows through the ONE economy.
   588→
   589→### 14.3 The ONE-economy contract (§14.5)
   590→
   591→`trackMerge` / `recordMergeRun` / `claimMergeEgg` / `seeCreature` are pure
   592→pipelines: quest bumps (`merges`, `mergeNew`, `mergeLeo`) and achievement
   593→XP all pass through `addXpPure` + `bumpQuests`, so the daily ring, the
   594→league and the streak can never disagree with the arcade. The quest board
   595→is exactly 3 rows with DISTINCT event types — `questsForDay` guarantees it.

   599→## 15. LEO EGGS — the surprise-egg arcade (v6)
   600→
   601→The flagship pure-fun game #2, built on blind-box psychology — the same
   602→hooks that keep a feed scroll going, tuned for small hands. You pick an
   603→egg, PRESS AND HOLD it (one thumb, zero skill), the three.js egg shakes,
   604→fissures spread, gold light teases — then it bursts and a friend joins
   605→the collection. Design brain: `src/lib/eggs.ts`; renderer
   606→`components/eggs/Egg3D.tsx` (three.js) + `EggGame.tsx` (GSAP timeline).
   607→
   608→### 15.1 The psychology (researched)
   609→
   610→| Hook (Hooked model) | Mechanic in LEO EGGS |
   611→| --- | --- |
   612→| Trigger | The Home card glows "Nest egg ready!"; the Royal Egg is free every morning; quest rows pull egg events |
   613→| Action | Pick an egg → hold-to-crack. No fail state, no skill gate — releasing early just pauses |
   614→| Variable reward | Rarity table (slot-machine schedule), near-miss gold tease, 1.4 s silhouette suspense, rarity-scaled fanfare + screen shake + haptics. Never a "miss": duplicates pay stars |
   615→| Investment | Album completion (silhouettes nag), Stage-2 growth (feed 2 extra copies), the nest keeps incubating after the app closes, streak days make eggs luckier |
   616→
   617→Key researched principles baked in:
   618→
   619→- **Blind-box reveal** — "maybe this is the rare one" is the engine; dopamine
   620→  peaks BEFORE the reveal, so the crack is a slow, touchable ritual and the
   621→  next egg is always one tap away (the infinite-feed affordance).
   622→- **Variable-ratio table** — Spark (free): 68/22/7.5/2/0.5 %, Rainbow (25★):
   623→  20/48/22/8/2 %, Royal (60★): 0/40/40/16/4 % (common/rare/epic/legend/mythic).
   624→- **Near-miss tease** — P(gold glow) per rarity: 15/45/80/100/100 %. Honest
   625→  but noisy: informative enough to chase, never a promise.
   626→- **Pity** — after 7 dry Spark eggs the 8th is forced rare-or-better, and the
   627→  countdown is VISIBLE (the pity meter). Losing streaks end; hope never does.
   628→- **Streak luck** — `+2.5%/streak-day` rare-or-better luck, capped at +12.5 %:
   629→  the lion app and the egg game now pull the same direction.
   630→
   631→### 15.2 The economy
   632→
   633→- 13 friends across 5 rarities (5 common, 4 rare, 2 epic, 1 legendary cub,
   634→  1 mythic star). All art from the sticker library — zero new assets.
   635→- Duplicates pay stars: 2/5/12/30/60 by rarity; a first discovery pays +5★
   636→  and +XP. Rarity XP (2/3/5/10/20) flows through the ONE economy.
   637→- Stars buy Rainbow (25★) / Royal (60★) eggs — the duplicate sink.
   638→- Growth: 3 hatches of one species → feed 2 extra → Stage 2 (golden frame,
   639→  +10 XP, pure pride).
   640→- Nest: a free Spark egg incubates in real time (`NEST_HATCH_MS` = 15 min,
   641→  `eggNestAt`); when it hatches the next one starts immediately — the app
   642→  is always growing something while it is closed.
   643→- Spark rest: 4 s between free eggs (≈ one reveal read) so the crack →
   644→  reveal → NEXT loop never stalls at its dopamine peak.
   645→- Quest events `eggs` / `eggNew` / `eggRare` joined the SAME daily board
   646→  (§14.3 contract still holds: 3 rows, distinct events).
   647→- Achievements: First Crack · Egg Explorer (6) · Golden Luck (legendary) ·
   648→  Nest Master (all 13) · Grow Up (first Stage 2).
   649→
   650→### 15.3 The three.js egg
   651→
   652→`Egg3D` renders one canvas: a speckled candy egg (generated canvas
   653→textures, no downloads), hemisphere + key + rim lights, drag-to-spin with
   654→inertia, hold-shake driven by crack progress, an alpha crack overlay that
   655→eases in, an additive gold glow sprite (the tease), and a burst — two
   656→shell halves with gravity + 90 round glow sparks (radial-gradient sprite
   657→particles, never squares). DPR capped at 2, everything disposed on
   658→unmount; props reach the loop through refs so a hold never re-renders.

   662→## 16. LEO FEED — the infinite surprise feed (v7)
   663→
   664→### 16.1 Why a feed, and what it copies
   665→
   666→The brief: *not* educational, "a game with the mind like an Instagram
   667→algorithm", something that keeps kids on the screen without a single
   668→question to answer. The feed format is the answer, and every mechanic
   669→has a named lineage:
   670→
   671→| Mechanic | Borrowed from | Why it works |
   672→| --- | --- | --- |
   673→| The endless card stream | Instagram/TikTok | A feed has no finish line — "the next one" is always one flick away, so play never hits a natural stop |
   674→| Variable-ratio rarity | Slot machines / gacha | Every card pays something; the brain keeps re-arming for the big one |
   675→| The unwrap ritual | Candy Crush Sugar Crush / gacha pulls | Dopamine peaks BEFORE the reveal — the shake is the product, the reveal is the receipt |
   676→| Near-miss gold flash | Skinner boxes / modern gacha | "SO CLOSE" is the strongest known re-grip in game design |
   677→| HEAT meter | Hype/train meters (Brawl Stars, TikTok streaks) | Momentum you can SEE; full meter = a guaranteed MEGA card |
   678→| Pity counter | Gacha pity systems (Genshin) | Dry spells always end; hope never does |
   679→| Affinity ("Leo picked this for you") | Recommender systems | The feed LEARNS which friends the kid loves and leans toward them |
   680→| Wall of Fame | Trading-card albums / Pokédex | Completion pull with visible faces nagging from the gaps |
   681→| Daily MEGA gift | Duolingo / daily-shop FOMO | The reason to open the app again tomorrow |
   682→
   683→There is no text to read anywhere in the loop: icons, colours and
   684→animation carry it. The "algorithm" wink is the chip on every card:
   685→*Leo picked this for you.*
   686→
   687→### 16.2 The math (lib/feed.ts)
   688→
   689→- **Rarity table** (variable ratio, same ladder as Leo Eggs):
   690→  common 55% · rare 25% · epic 13% · legend 5.5% · mythic 1.5%.
   691→- **Streak luck**: −0.5 percentage points off the roll per streak day
   692→  (cap 10) — a longer flame bends the table toward shiny, so the lion
   693→  app and the feed pull in the same direction.
   694→- **Near-miss**: a common that rolled <3 points under the epic/legend/
   695→  mythic gate flashes gold mid-shake (65% of qualifying rolls — honest
   696→  but noisy, pattern-hunger needs variety).
   697→- **Pity**: `feedPity` counts cards since the last epic+; at
   698→  `PITY_MAX = 9` the next card is forced epic-or-better. Dots are always
   699→  visible under the card.
   700→- **HEAT**: shiny reveals +1 heat (commons keep the warmth), at
   701→  `HEAT_MAX = 5` the NEXT card is a MEGA card — forced epic+ — and the
   702→  meter cools to 0. Best heat is kept in `feedBestHeat` for the
   703→  achievement.
   704→- **Affinity** (the recommender): every sighting multiplies that
   705→  friend's pull weight by `AFFINITY_STEP = 1.35`, capped ×6 — favourites
   706→  recur, but rarity is always governed by the table above, so love
   707→  never becomes a monopoly.
   708→- **Payouts**: rarity XP 2/3/5/10/20 through `addXp` (daily ring, league,
   709→  streak, quests — a pull IS a played day); duplicates pay Spark into
   710→  the same purse Leo Eggs uses (2/5/12/30/60 + 5 for a first discovery).
   711→- **Daily MEGA gift**: one free forced-epic+ card per local day
   712→  (`feedMegaDay`), +10 XP on claim — the Home card pulses when it waits.
   713→- **Comeback warmth**: `comebackLuck` reads `feedPullDay` and adds up to
   714→  +8 luck after days away.
   715→
   716→### 16.3 The ritual (FeedGame.tsx)
   717→
   718→The unwrap is a **GSAP timeline**: the card wobbles harder and faster
   719→(amplitude 2→9 px, ~130 ms per beat), a radial glow breathes in the
   720→rarity colour, and the ritual lasts longer for higher rarity
   721→(0.85 s common → 2.4 s mythic) — anticipation scales with reward.
   722→A near-miss flashes "SO CLOSE!" at peak tension. The reveal pops with a
   723→spring, pays its XP/Spark chips, and legend/mythic pull Leo on screen
   724→with a fanfare / golden roar. Swipe up (or NEXT) flicks the card away
   725→and the next one rises from below — with the edge of the card after it
   726→peeking at the bottom, the infinite-scroll affordance.
   727→
   728→`FeedSky` (three.js) drifts a real 3D galaxy behind the cards —
   729→200-point dust field + 14 spinning octahedron stars and icosahedron
   730→gems, DPR capped at 1.5, render loop paused when the tab hides,
   731→everything disposed on unmount. three.js and GSAP both ride in lazy
   732→chunks; phones never download them until the feed opens.
   733→
   734→### 16.4 Integration — the ONE economy (store v7)
   735→
   736→`trackFeed(plan)` is ONE pure pipeline built from a single snapshot:
   737→Wall of Fame (`feedAlbum`), affinity (`feedAffinity`), heat (`feedHeat`
   738→/ `feedBestHeat`), pity (`feedPity`), pulls (`feedPulls`, `feedPullDay`),
   739→Spark (`eggStars` — the shared purse), the quests (`pulls` / `feedNew` /
   740→`feedRare` joined the SAME 3-row daily board) and the rarity XP through
   741→`addXpPure` — so streaks, leagues, the daily ring and player levels all
   742→advance while the kid "just plays". Achievements: First Swipe · Feed on
   743→Fire · Golden Feed · Feed Dreamer · Feed Explorer (6) · Feed Master
   744→(all 16).
   745→
   746→### 16.5 The pattern question is gone
   747→
   748→The old "What comes next in the pattern?" mini-game (the square-#9b5de5
   749→screen) is deleted from the question factory, the play screen and both
   750→simulation scripts. A four-year-old never has to parse an abstract
   751→sequence again — pattern-hunting moved to where it belongs: the feed,
   752→where the pattern is "maybe THIS one is the golden one".
